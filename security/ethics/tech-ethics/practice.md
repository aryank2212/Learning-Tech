# Practice: Tech Ethics

Work through the levels in order. Spend at least 30 minutes on a task before looking anything up. At the end, a short quiz checks the key ideas, and each answer is hidden until you click it.

How to check yourself: write tests for each task, or compare your output with a known answer. For maths and theory, check by hand and then with code.

## Level 1 — Easy (warm-up)

- [ ] List three groups affected by a face-recognition system.
- [ ] Write a one-paragraph privacy notice for an app.
- [ ] Name one dark pattern and how to fix it.

## Level 2 — Medium (apply it)

- [ ] Compute fairness metrics on a dataset and explain the trade-off you find.
- [ ] Write a model card for a model you trained.
- [ ] Apply the ethics checklist to one of your projects.

## Level 3 — Hard (build and explain)

- [ ] Red-team a chatbot for two hours and write a harms report.
- [ ] Analyse two case studies, each through two frameworks.

## Quick quiz

Answer each question out loud or on paper first, then open the answer.

1. Name two fairness metrics.

   <details><summary>Show answer</summary>

   Demographic parity and equalised odds. Calibration is a third.

   </details>

2. What is data minimisation?

   <details><summary>Show answer</summary>

   Collecting only the data you need for a stated purpose.

   </details>

3. Why can't every fairness definition hold at once?

   <details><summary>Show answer</summary>

   When groups have different base rates, results such as those of Kleinberg et al. and Chouldechova show that calibration and equal error rates can't all be satisfied.

   </details>

4. What is a model card?

   <details><summary>Show answer</summary>

   A short document describing a model's intended use, training data, performance across groups, and limits.

   </details>
